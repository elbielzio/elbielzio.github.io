## Part 1: Lesson Plan

### Digital Pedagogy in Practice Lesson Plan

**DH8991 Introduction to Digital Humanities: Week 16**

Dustin Thomas and Gabriel Santos de Oliveira

**Class Topic:** Digital Archaeology and the accessibility of 3D technologies

**Learning Outcomes:** Demonstrate the accessibility and utility of 3D technologies via case studies and applications in digital archaeology, emphasizing the collaborative and improvisational opportunities of platforms such as RealityScan and Scaniverse.

**Preparation:**

- Students should read the assigned readings (listed below)
- Students should bring to class an object of significance they would like to collect 3D data on (ideal objects include anything that is relevant to their research)
- Students should download Scaniverse and/or Reality Scan on their iPad, iPhone, or Android phone (necessary for the hands-on activity; Scaniverse available on iPad or iPhone Pro models only)

**Readings:**

Garstki, Kevin. 2024. “Digital Archaeology.” In *Encyclopedia of Archaeology (Second Edition)*, edited by Efthymia Nikita and Thilo Rehren. Academic Press. [https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-323-90799-6.00176-2](https://doi.org/10.1016/B978-0-323-90799-6.00176-2).

Álvarez Larrain, Alina, Catriel Greco, and Myriam Tarragó. 2021. “Participatory Mapping and UAV Photogrammetry as Complementary Techniques for Landscape Archaeology Studies: An Example from North‐western Argentina.” *Archaeological Prospection* 28 (1): 47–61. [https://doi.org/10.1002/arp.1794](https://doi.org/10.1002/arp.1794).

Rihani, Nemeh. 2023. “Interactive Immersive Experience: Digital Technologies for Reconstruction and Experiencing Temple of Bel Using Crowdsourced Images and 3D Photogrammetric Processes.” *International Journal of Architectural Computing* 21 (4): 730–56. [https://doi.org/10.1177/14780771231168224](https://doi.org/10.1177/14780771231168224).

**Lecture outline (30 minutes):**

- What is Digital Archaeology?;
- Review of Methods (Structured/Linked Open Data; GIS, LiDAR, Photogrammetry);
- Collaborative Digital Archaeology (readings as case studies)

**Hands-on Activity outline:**

1. Arrange the classroom to have 3-4 “stations” using flat stationary surfaces.
2. Students should split into groups of 2-3. Groups should represent a mix of devices and access to either Scaniverse or Reality Scan
3. Students will set up their preferred scanning objects in the middle of the classroom tables. The tables and objects should be circumnavigable and visible from as many angles as possible.
4. Each group should alternate using Scaniverse and RealityScan to scan the objects using the guided workflows provided by each app. (See linked videos for further tutorial guidance: [https://youtu.be/HVkvHZCmVjU?si=Od6\_L2mDZmHAKgFb](https://youtu.be/HVkvHZCmVjU?si=Od6_L2mDZmHAKgFb) and [https://youtu.be/ELXLFMjLOx8?si=Ri9w50u\_-Q6v1Lhw](https://youtu.be/ELXLFMjLOx8?si=Ri9w50u_-Q6v1Lhw)
5. Instructors will rotate around the room to check-in.
6. The remaining 5-10 minutes of class should be devoted to addressing the following discussion sections:
    - What was the quality of the models that each group scanned? Did these models meet or fall short of the expectations of group members?
    - Were the workflows and interfaces of the apps used easy to follow or was further guidance necessary?
    - Do class members feel that these apps were accessible and can they think of ways this (potentially) more accessible method of 3D data capture could be used in their research approaches?
