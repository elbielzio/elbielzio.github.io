The selected corpus consists of five texts that I utilized for my second-year essay in 2025, where I examined Amazonian Archaeology and Relational Landscapes. The results reveal notable patterns in the usage of specific terms, including years, mercury, human, dark, Amazon, and Indigenous.

Analysis of the document segments highlights the frequent occurrence of the term mercury, reflecting the ongoing environmental crisis affecting Indigenous populations in Amazonia. Amazonian soil, particularly in areas impacted by illegal mining, has been heavily exploited, resulting in the release of substantial quantities of mercury into both soil and water, which has caused the deaths of thousands of Indigenous people. Some of these affected regions consist of Amazonian Dark Earth. This dark earth contains high levels of carbonized material produced through the slow burning of organic matter, such as plants, seeds, and roots. Research integrating archaeological and ethnographic evidence demonstrates that these soils were intentionally created by Indigenous communities, who added ashes and charred residues from daily activities to the ground over many generations (Schmidt et al. 2021).

The compiled results of this brief analysis using Voyant indicate that mercury-related issues, driven by illegal mining, have not only resulted in the deaths of contemporary Indigenous people in Amazonia but have also contaminated ancestral soils constructed by Amazonian ancestors.
